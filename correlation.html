Methodology

Use complete X, Y pairs. Let Sxx = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)², Syy = Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)², and Sxy = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ).

Pearson correlation

r=SxySxxSyy,t=rn−21−r2,df=n−2r=\frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}},\qquad t=\frac{r\sqrt{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}},\qquad df=n-2

The test of H₀: ρ = 0 assumes independent observations from a bivariate normal population. On the correlation scale, the exact null density is proportional to (1 − r²)(n − 4)/2 for −1 < r < 1. The standardized view uses the equivalent t distribution; this transformation is nonlinear.

Confidence interval for ρ

Fisher’s approximation uses zr = atanh(r) and SE(zr) = 1/√(n − 3), requiring more than three pairs. For a two-tailed test:

[tanh(zr−zcrit×SE),tanh(zr+zcrit×SE)][\tanh(z_r-z_{\text{crit}}\times SE),\;\tanh(z_r+z_{\text{crit}}\times SE)]

zcrit is the 1 − α/2 standard normal quantile. One-tailed bounds use the 1 − α quantile: the upper bound is 1 for a right-tailed test, and the lower bound is −1 for a left-tailed test. This approximate interval can disagree with the exact test near a cutoff, especially for small samples. Its green line estimates ρ; on the t view, the bounds are transformed only for display.

Simple linear regression of Y on X

y^=b0+b1x,b1=SxySxx,b0=y¯−b1x¯\hat y=b_0+b_1x,\qquad b_1=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}},\qquad b_0=\bar y-b_1\bar x

Residual SD is se = √[Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²/(n − 2)]. The slope SE is se/√Sxx. To test H₀: β₁ = 0, use t = b₁/SE with n − 2 degrees of freedom. R² = r².

Inference assumes a linear conditional mean with independent, normally distributed errors of equal variance. The lower chart shows the null t distribution; the slope scale multiplies t coordinates by the estimated slope SE. It is a reference curve using the observed SE, not an exact sampling density of the unstandardized slope.

Confidence interval for β₁

[b1−tcrit×SE,b1+tcrit×SE][b_1-t_{\text{crit}}\times SE,\;b_1+t_{\text{crit}}\times SE]

tcrit is the 1 − α/2 quantile for a two-tailed test, or the 1 − α quantile for a one-tailed test. A right-tailed interval extends to ∞; a left-tailed interval starts at −∞. The interval estimates the population slope, not individual Y values.

p-value and decision

The p-value covers the null curve beyond the observed statistic in the selected direction, or beyond its magnitude in both tails. Reject H₀ when p-value < α. Confidence level is 100(1 − α)%. Correlation and regression alone do not establish causation.
