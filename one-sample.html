Methodology

One-sample z-test

σ is the known population SD; μ₀ is the hypothesized mean.

SE=σnSE=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}
z=x¯−μ0SEz=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{SE}

One-sample t-test

s is the sample SD; μ₀ is the hypothesized mean.

SE=snSE=\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}
t=x¯−μ0SEt=\frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{SE}
df=n−1df=n-1

Confidence interval

Confidence level is 100(1 − α)%. The formulas use the positive critical value (zcrit or tcrit). For two-tailed tests, it is the 1 − α/2 quantile. For right-tailed tests, it is the 1 − α quantile and the interval extends from the lower bound to ∞. For left-tailed tests, it extends from −∞ to the upper bound.

[xˉ−zcrit×SE,  xˉ+zcrit×SE][\bar{x}-z_{\text{crit}}\times SE,\;\bar{x}+z_{\text{crit}}\times SE]
[xˉ−tcrit×SE,  xˉ+tcrit×SE][\bar{x}-t_{\text{crit}}\times SE,\;\bar{x}+t_{\text{crit}}\times SE]

p-value and critical values

The p-value is the area beyond the observed statistic in the selected tail, or both tails for a two-tailed test. Critical values leave α in the selected tail, or α/2 in each tail. Reject H₀ when p-value < α.
